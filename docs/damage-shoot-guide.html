Damaged-card shoot: illustrated guide

Teach the gate what a damaged card looks like. You damage printed specimen cards by hand and photograph each one and add them to the 152 photos you already have. About 152 new photos across the two card layouts. The pictures below are drawings on your real card faces, not photos: they show what each defect should look like and where it sits against the text zone (purple dashed box).

Why every damaged card gets one clean photo first: most of your existing 152 photos show the card inside the clear pouch. If every undamaged example is pouched and every damaged one is bare, the model learns "no pouch = damaged". One bare, undamaged photo of each card, taken before you damage it, gives the model bare cards that are fine. This is the one photo that must not be skipped.

Time is an estimate: about 2 hours for everything, including damaging the cards. You can do one layout one day and the other the next. If you run short, do all the SEVERE cards first, they matter most to the next step.

2. Before you start

  • Printed cards cut out, copy number written on the back of each (ID-01 ... ID-24, BANK-01 ... BANK-24).
  • A pen or pencil (for curling), scissors, a flat desk, and two different surfaces (for example a wood table and a dark keyboard or mat).
  • Phone: Photo mode, Live Photo off, flash off, no filters, portrait mode off. Tap to focus on the card.
  • Light: daylight or a lamp from the side. Side light makes creases show up, but keep it off the text so there is no glare patch on it.
  • No sleeve or pouch. Keep every card bare for this shoot (sleeves are a separate problem, out of this iteration).
  • Specimen cards only. No real document, no other card, no business card, no personal mail in the background. Keep your face out of frame.

3. Golden rules

  1. Clean photo FIRST. Photograph the card bare and undamaged, then damage it. Then shoot it at once: curls and bows relax within a minute.
  2. One problem per photo. No glare on the text, no finger over the text, no blur, all four corners in frame. Only the damage is wrong.
  3. Damage must be visible on the phone screen. If you cannot see it, press harder and retake.
  4. Text stays legible in MILD cards and is crossed (but not destroyed) in SEVERE ones.
  5. One card, one damage. Never damage a card twice. Use the next numbered card.

4. Where the text zone is

MILD means the damage stays away from the purple box. SEVERE means it crosses it.

5. The eight damage scenarios

Left picture: the ID card. Right picture: the bank card. Same defect, same recipe. Purple dashed box = text zone.

Fold, mild mild

How to make it
Fold one corner over, about 12 mm each way (the bottom-left corner, as drawn). Press the fold flat with a fingernail, then let go.
The photo must show
Yes: The folded flap and its fold line are visible. The corner is NOT touching the text zone.
Common mistake
No: Folding it so far that the flap covers printed text. That is no longer 'mild'.

Fold, severe severe

How to make it
Fold the card in half down the middle (short edges together), press hard, then open it back to roughly a right angle and lay it down so it stays bent.
The photo must show
Yes: A hard centre fold line running through the text zone, with the two halves at different angles to the camera (one half looks narrower and darker).
Common mistake
No: Shooting while the card is folded flat shut (you see half a card), or fully flattened out again (the fold has relaxed, shoot it bent).

Crease, mild mild

How to make it
Bend the card gently across its width below the text zone, press one crease line with a fingernail, and flatten it back. One line only.
The photo must show
Yes: One clear light/dark crease line crossing the card in a blank area, away from the text.
Common mistake
No: A crease so faint it cannot be seen in the photo. If you cannot see it on the phone screen, press harder and retake.

Crease, severe severe

How to make it
Make two hard creases that cross each other, one down the middle and one across, so the text zone sits on top of the crossing. Flatten the card back out.
The photo must show
Yes: Two crossing crease lines through the text zone, each with a visible bright edge and dark edge.
Common mistake
No: Creases that stop before the text zone. Both lines must cross it.

Tear, mild mild

How to make it
Nick the edge with scissors or tear a small notch, about 5 mm wide, in the top edge, away from the text zone.
The photo must show
Yes: A small bite out of the edge with the desk showing through it, text zone untouched.
Common mistake
No: Cutting it with a clean straight scissor line. Tear it so the edge is ragged and shows pale paper fibre.

Tear, severe severe

How to make it
Tear the bottom-right corner off (about 25 mm, ragged) AND tear a 40 to 100 mm slit in from the right edge so it runs toward, and for the bank card into, the text zone.
The photo must show
Yes: A missing corner and a slit, both with ragged edges, the slit reaching the text zone.
Common mistake
No: Tearing right through the text, so it cannot be read at all. The aim is damage over legible text, not destroyed text.

Curl / peel, mild mild

How to make it
Lift the bottom-right corner by rolling it over a pen for a few seconds, then lay the card flat. The corner should stay up about 10 mm off the desk.
The photo must show
Yes: A lifted corner showing the paper's back side and a small shadow, text zone flat and untouched.
Common mistake
No: Shooting after the corner has settled flat again. Curl and shoot straight away.

Curl / bow, severe severe

How to make it
Roll the whole card around a pen along its length for 15 seconds, release, and lay it down so it bows up in the middle and rocks on the desk.
The photo must show
Yes: The whole card visibly bowed: shaded bands across the face, curved edges, a shadow under the lifted middle.
Common mistake
No: A card that has relaxed flat. If it lies flat, roll it again. Shoot within a minute, the bow relaxes.

6. Hard negatives: cards that look odd but are NOT damaged

These stop the model from flagging every curved or tilted card. Shoot 3 photos of each card, no clean-before needed. (Bow: two cards. Steep tilt: two cards.)

Gentle bow (NOT damage) no damage

How to make it
Flex the card slightly between your fingers and lay it down so it sits a little curved. No crease anywhere.
Why
Label: no damage. This teaches the model that slightly curved is normal.

Steep tilt (NOT damage) no damage

How to make it
A clean flat card photographed at a steep angle (about 45 degrees), so the shape looks squashed.
Why
Label: no damage. A tilted clean card is not a damaged one; this is the exact trap the mask-shape test fell into.

7. The shot sequence for every damaged card (4 photos)

  1. Photo 1, CLEAN BEFORE. Bare card, undamaged, flat and square on the desk (A). Before you touch it.
  2. Damage the card with the recipe above.
  3. Photo 2: flat and square again (A).
  4. Photo 3: turn it 20 to 30 degrees and move it to the other surface (B).
  5. Photo 4: hold it in your hand in different light (C), fingers on the edges only, never over the damage or the text zone.

Landscape for most photos, a few in portrait. The card should fill about a third to two thirds of the frame width, all four corners visible.

10. Getting the photos in

One folder per physical card, named with the copy number and the plan, with the photos in the order you took them (photo 1 is always the clean BEFORE). AirDrop each card's photos to a folder like:

~/Downloads/damage/ID-03-fold-severe/   ~/Downloads/damage/BANK-17-hardneg-bow/

Do not rename the photos. Tick the card off in the log as you go. Raw photos stay out of git (local/ is ignored). The next step teaches the ingest script about these folders; until then just keep them as they are.

One honest limit: printed paper does not fold or tear like real plastic ID. This set teaches the idea of damage on paper specimens; it is a seed, not proof the gate catches worn real documents. Real-world damage frames come later, from the bypass queue.
